Neonatal Reflexes

How Babies Perceive Themselves

🪞 The Rouge Test (Amsterdam, 1972)

A dab of red makeup is secretly placed just below the infant's eye. The infant is then placed in front of a mirror. If they reach for their own face (not the mirror surface), they pass — classed as a self-recognizer.

Timeline

18 months: Some pass
24 months: Majority pass
⚠️ Limitation: May measure mirror understanding, not just self-awareness

External vs. Internal Perception

External: Well-researched, visual, how babies see the world around them.

Internal (Self): Under-researched, requires touch study, how babies sense their own body.

👶 Early Evidence of Self-Perception — Earlier Than 18 Months!

Birth
Newborn Synchrony
Filippetti et al. (2013): Newborns look longer at a face being stroked at the same time their own cheek is stroked. Some body awareness is present from birth.
4 mo
Visual Self-Recognition of Legs
Bahrick & Watson (1985): 4-month-olds can identify their own moving legs on video vs. another child's legs or a time-delayed video of themselves.
6 mo
Touch Becomes External-World Mapped
Touch begins to be located in external space, not just on the body. This is why 6-month-olds make errors when limbs are crossed.
18–24 mo
Mirror Self-Recognition (Rouge Test)
The milestone most people know — but NOT the start of self-perception. It is one milestone in a long developmental chain beginning at birth.

🤲 Bremner et al. (2008) — The "Buzzer" Experiment

Vibrotactile buzzer placed in one palm. Task: look at and move the correct buzzed hand. Key variable: hands uncrossed vs. crossed over body midline.

6.5-Month-Olds

Uncrossed: ✓ Good
Crossed: ✗ Many errors

Can move the correct hand, but struggle to FIND it with their eyes when on the wrong side.

10-Month-Olds

Uncrossed: ✓ Good
Crossed: ✓ Equally good

Posture-independent body map from midline crossing experience + brain maturation (Rigato et al., 2014).

🦶 Begum Ali et al. (2015) — The "Tickled Feet" Study

Feet tickled with legs either uncrossed or crossed.

4-Month-Olds

High accuracy BOTH crossed & uncrossed ✓✓

Body-centric: "My foot feels a tickle." Crossing doesn't confuse them.

6-Month-Olds

Uncrossed: ~70% ✓ | Crossed: ~50% (chance) ✗

External-centric: brain maps touch to space. Right foot on left side = confusion.
🚨 Counter-intuitive exam trap!4-month-olds OUTPERFORM 6-month-olds when crossed — NOT because they are more developed, but because they lack the external-world mapping that causes the confusion!

Sensorimotor Development

⚙️ The Perception–Action Link

Motor abilities cannot be studied in isolation from perceptual skills. Motor tasks are essentially about learning how to best link perception and action.

Walking requires:

• Proprioception: Muscles → brain: which foot is forward
• Distance senses: Vision/hearing → where to walk
• Vision & posture: Reference point for gravity

Old vs. New View

Old: Motor dev = sequence (crawling → walking)

New: Emerging ability to control motor behavior with respect to sensory information

🧱 The Building Block Theory

Reflexes are NOT biological leftovers. They provide behaviors that can be (1) adapted to new actions and (2) linked together to form adult skills.

1
Reflex Stage
Involuntary response (e.g., rooting when cheek is touched)
2
Practice / Adaptation
Infant repeats the behavior in various contexts
3
Integration
Behavior linked with other sensory inputs (vision)
4
Voluntary Control
Initial reflex replaced by purposeful, controlled action

🩺 Brazelton Neonatal Assessment Scale — S.M.I.L.E.

A "total baby check-up" combining reflex checks with sensory, motor, and state-regulation assessments. Used to identify infants at risk for developmental problems.

  • SSensory/Perceptual — orientation to sights and sounds
  • MMotor Development — evaluation of muscle tone
  • IInvoluntary Response Control — is brain properly controlling reflexes?
  • LLearning / Habituation — familiarity with sensory stimuli
  • EEmotional States — ability to regulate states, soothability
Reflex Red FlagsToo weak · Too strong · Staying too long → any of these = neurological concern.

Memory Aids & Exam Traps

🍼 Feeding Reflexes — "Babies Really Suck"

  • BBabkin — press BOTH palms → mouth opens, eyes close, head to midline
  • RRooting — stroke cheek → turns toward stimulus (disappears awake ~3-4 mo, asleep ~7-8 mo!)
  • SSucking — object in mouth → rhythmic sucking (reflex until ~4 mo, then voluntary)

⚡ Primitive Reflexes — "Moro Grasps Galant's Tonic Fist"

  • MMoro — sudden drop → arms OUT then IN, fists clench, cry (4–6 mo)
  • GGalant — stroke SIDE of spine → trunk curves TOWARD stimulus
  • GGrasp (Palmar) — press palm → fingers curl & grip (3–6 mo)
  • TTonic Neck (ATNR) — head turns right → RIGHT arm extends, left flexes ("fencing")

🩺 Brazelton Scale — S.M.I.L.E.

  • SSensory/Perceptual
  • MMotor — muscle tone
  • IInvoluntary control — reflex regulation
  • LLearning/Habituation
  • EEmotional states — soothability

🔑 "Do Not Confuse" Quick Reference

Concept Avs.Concept B
Babinski: heel-to-toe → big toe UP, others FAN≠Plantar grasp: ball of foot → toes CURL DOWN
Rooting disappears AWAKE at 3–4 months≠Rooting disappears ASLEEP at 7–8 months
Rouge Test: visual self-recognition (18–24 months)≠Tactile localization: body mapping (birth–10 months)
4-month-olds: body-centric → better crossed-limb accuracy≠6-month-olds: external-centric → worse when crossed
CNS depression: weakens ALL major reflexes≠Spinal defect: affects SPECIFIC limb reflexes
